=== sections/04_upper_blade.tex
1: \section{The upper bound}
2: \label{sec:least-safe-upper_blade}
3: 
4: \Cref{sec:least-safe-lcm_criterion} turned the absence of a witness below $B$ into
5: the divisibility $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \mid G(N)$. At $B = \ell(N) - 1$
6: it says that $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell(N)-1)$ divides $G(N)$, and $G(N)$
7: itself divides $N(N-1)$ (\cref{thm:least-safe-dvd-mul-sub-one}), so a least common
8: multiple of size about $N^2$ has to be large. This section makes that
9: quantitative for every odd composite, from one elementary inequality on
10: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)$, and the inequality comes from a binomial
11: coefficient: every prime power dividing $\binom{2n}{n}$ is at most $2n$, so
12: $\binom{2n}{n}$ divides $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n)$, while
13: $4^n \le 2n\binom{2n}{n}$; together, $2^{2n} \le 2n \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n)$,
14: which the three lemmas below bring to the form spent at $y = \ell(N) - 1$.
15: 
16: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{centralBinom\_dvd\_lcm\_Icc}
17: \label{lem:least-safe-central-binom-dvd-lcm}
18: Let $n \ge 1$. Then $\binom{2n}{n}$ divides
19: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n)$.
20: \end{lemma}
21: \begin{proof}
22: Write $C = \binom{2n}{n}$ and $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n)$, both nonzero.
23: Divisibility of positive integers is decided prime by prime, so fix a prime $p$:
24: the valuation of a binomial coefficient obeys $p^{v_p(C)} \le 2n$
25: (\lean{pow\_factorization\_centralBinom\_le}), so $p^{v_p(C)}$ is a positive
26: integer at most $2n$, hence one of the members of $[1,2n]$, and $L$ is divisible
27: by each of its members. Therefore $p^{v_p(C)} \mid L$, that is
28: $v_p(C) \le v_p(L)$, at every prime $p$, and $C \mid L$.
29: \end{proof}
30: 
31: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{four\_pow\_le\_mul\_lcm\_Icc}
32: \label{lem:least-safe-four-pow-le-mul-lcm}
33: Let $n \ge 1$. Then $4^n \le 2n \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n)$.
34: \end{lemma}
35: \begin{proof}
36: For $n \ge 1$, $4^n \le 2n\binom{2n}{n}$
37: (\lean{Nat.four\_pow\_le\_two\_mul\_self\_mul\_centralBinom}), the elementary bound
38: that Erd\H{o}s's proof of Bertrand's postulate turns on, and
39: $\binom{2n}{n} \le \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n)$ by
40: \cref{lem:least-safe-central-binom-dvd-lcm}, since a divisor of a positive integer
41: is at most it. Multiplying the second inequality by $2n$ and chaining the two,
42: \[
43:   4^n \;\le\; 2n\binom{2n}{n} \;\le\; 2n\,\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n).
44: \]
45: \end{proof}
46: 
47: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{two\_pow\_pred\_le\_mul\_lcm}
48: \label{lem:least-safe-two-pow-pred-le-mul-lcm}
49: For every $y \ge 1$, $2^{y-1} \le y \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)$.
50: \end{lemma}
51: \begin{proof}
52: Split on the parity of $y \ge 1$. If $y = 2n$ is even then $n \ge 1$, and
53: \cref{lem:least-safe-four-pow-le-mul-lcm} at $n$ reads
54: $2^{2n} \le 2n \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2n) = 2n \cdot
55: \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)$; writing $2^{2n} = 2 \cdot 2^{2n-1}$ and
56: cancelling the factor $2$ gives $2^{y-1} \le n \cdot
57: \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)$, and $n \le y$ gives the claim. If $y = 1$ the
58: claim is $1 \le 1$. If $y = 2(n+1)+1 \ge 3$ is odd, \cref{lem:least-safe-four-pow-le-mul-lcm} at $n+1$ reads $2^{y-1} = 2^{2(n+1)} \le 2(n+1) \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2(n+1))$, and both factors grow when $2(n+1)$ is replaced by the odd endpoint $y$: $2(n+1) \le y$, and $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2(n+1)) \le \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)$ because the shorter one divides the longer (\lean{lcm\_Icc\_mono}).
59: \end{proof}
60: 
61: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{leastSafeModulus\_le}
62: \label{thm:least-safe-modulus-le}
63: Let $N$ be an odd composite. Then
64: \[
65:   \ell(N) \;\le\; 2\log_2\bigl(N(N-1)\bigr) + 3 .
66: \]
67: \end{theorem}
68: \begin{proof}
69: Let $G = G(N)$ and $T = N(N-1)$, so that $\ell(N) = \ell(G)$ is the least
70: non-divisor of $G$. For an odd composite $N$ the invariant is a positive integer
71: (\cref{thm:least-safe-pos}, \lean{residueGcd\_pos}), which is what makes
72: $\ell(G)$ defined, and $\ell(N) \ge 2$
73: (\cref{prop:least-safe-modulus-ge-two}, \lean{two\_le\_leastSafeModulus}), so
74: $y = \ell(N) - 1 \ge 1$ and
75: \cref{lem:least-safe-two-pow-pred-le-mul-lcm} at that $y$ reads
76: \[
77:   2^{\ell(N)-2} \;\le\; (\ell(N)-1)\,\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell(N)-1).
78: \]
79: 
80: Two divisors of $G$ bound the two factors on the right. At $B = \ell(N) - 1$,
81: \cref{thm:least-safe-lcm-before} (\lean{lcm\_before\_leastSafe\_dvd}) gives
82: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell(N)-1) \mid G$; and $\ell(N) - 1$ is an integer
83: $m$ with $1 \le m < \ell(N)$, so it divides $G$ by
84: \cref{lem:least-safe-modulus-below-dvd} (\lean{dvd\_of\_lt\_leastSafeModulus}).
85: Separately $G \mid T$ (\cref{thm:least-safe-dvd-mul-sub-one},
86: \lean{residueGcd\_dvd\_mul\_sub\_one}), and $T > 0$ because $N \ge 3$. A divisor
87: of a positive integer is at most it, so each of these two divisors of $G$ is at most $T$:
88: \[
89:   (\ell(N)-1)\,\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell(N)-1) \;\le\; T \cdot T = T^2 .
90: \]
91: 
92: Two elementary facts about $\log_2$ are all that is left: $2^k \le n$ with
93: $n > 0$ gives $k \le \log_2 n$ (\lean{le\_log2\_of\_pow\_le}), and
94: $\log_2(T^2) \le 2\log_2 T + 1$ (\lean{log2\_sq\_le}). As $T^2 > 0$ they give
95: $\ell(N) - 2 \le \log_2(T^2) \le 2\log_2 T + 1$, that is
96: \[
97:   \ell(N) \;\le\; 2\log_2\bigl(N(N-1)\bigr) + 3 .
98: \]
99: \end{proof}
100: 
101: The bound is also exported as \lean{leastSafeModulus\_residueGcd\_le} \lv.
102: 
103: The bound above is not the sharpest available. For every odd composite $N$,
104: $\ell(N) \le \lfloor \log_2 N / 2 \rfloor + \log_2(\log_2 N + 2) + 3$; and for
105: squarefree $N$ with at least three prime factors, in particular for every
106: Carmichael number,
107: $\ell(N) \le \lfloor \log_2 N / 3 \rfloor + \log_2(\log_2 N + 2) + 3$
108: (\cite{Companion-residue-gcd}, \lean{leastSafeModulus\_le\_half\_log2} and
109: \lean{leastSafeModulus\_le\_third\_log2}). Both are quoted, not used.
110: 
111: At the running example, the inequality at $y = \ell(N) - 1 = 6$ reads
112: $2^5 = 32 \le 6 \cdot 60 = 360$, and the bound itself allows much more:
113: $2^{165} \le N(N-1) < 2^{166}$, so $\log_2\bigl(N(N-1)\bigr) = 165$ and
114: \cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-le} gives $\ell(N) \le 2 \cdot 165 + 3 = 333$
115: against a true value of $7$: the bound holds with much room to spare.

77: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{leastSafeModulus\_sixty}
78: \label{lem:least-safe-sixty}
79: $\ell(60) = 7$.
80: \end{lemma}
81: \begin{proof}
82: Since $60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$, each of $2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ divides $60$, while
83: $7 \nmid 60$. The least integer $M \ge 2$ that does not divide $60$ is therefore
84: $7$, and by \cref{def:least-safe-modulus} that integer is $\ell(60)$.
85: \end{proof}

87: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{exists\_leastSafeModulus\_eq\_seven}
88: \label{thm:least-safe-exists-seven}
89: There is an odd composite $N$ with $\ell(N) = 7$.
90: \end{theorem}
91: \begin{proof}
92: Take the first row of \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder}: the member of
93: \cref{ex:least-safe-sixty}, at $m = 29$ and $K = 6486240$, with the three factors
94: $P = 188100961$, $Q = 194587201$ and $R = 201073441$. It is odd and at least $3$,
95: as every member of the consecutive family is. The three factors are prime, and
96: they are small enough for trial division in the kernel to settle it
97: (\cref{sec:least-safe-certificates}). By \cref{lem:least-safe-consec-korselt} (at $m = 29$, $K = 6486240$:
98: $2K+3 = 29 \cdot 447327$, $K = 30 \cdot 216208$, $2K+2m+1 = 31 \cdot 418469$) the
99: Korselt divisibilities $P - 1 = mK$, $Q - 1 = (m+1)K$ and $R - 1 = (m+2)K$ divide
100: $N - 1$; $m = 29$ is odd and $K = 6486240$ is even with $K \ge m + 3$, so the closed form of
101: \cref{thm:least-safe-consec-closed-form} applies,
102: and the divisors of $G(N)$ are the common divisors of $(m+1)^2 = 900$,
103: $(P - m^2)/2 = 94050060$ and $(R - (m+2)^2)/2 = 100536240$, whose gcd is $60$.
104: Hence $G(N) = 60$, as the value theorem
105: \lean{residueGcd\_consec\_record\_eq\_sixty} records. Were $N$ prime, $G(N)$ would
106: be $0$ (\cref{thm:least-safe-zero-iff-prime},
107: \lean{residueGcd\_eq\_zero\_iff\_prime}), and $G(N)$ is not $0$; so $N$ is composite, and
108: $G(N) = 60$ is positive, which is what makes $\ell(N)$ defined at that value. By
109: \cref{lem:least-safe-sixty} the least non-divisor of $60$ is $7$. Hence
110: $\ell(N) = 7$.
111: \end{proof}

120: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{exists\_leastSafeModulus\_eq\_eight}
121: \label{thm:least-safe-exists-eight}
122: There is an odd composite $N$ with $\ell(N) = 8$.
123: \end{theorem}
124: \begin{proof}
125: Take the second row of \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder}: its member $N$, the product
126: of the three factors $P = 4932634707001$, $Q = 4956235830001$ and
127: $R = 4979836953001$. The factors are prime; what makes that statement
128: theorem-grade is the certificate machinery of
129: \cref{sec:least-safe-certificates}.
130: 
131: The closed form of the invariant on the consecutive family
132: (\cref{thm:least-safe-consec-closed-form}, \lean{consec\_dvd\_residueGcd\_iff}) applies to a
133: member whose three factors are prime and whose Korselt divisibilities
134: $P - 1 = mK$, $Q - 1 = (m+1)K$ and $R - 1 = (m+2)K$ divide $N - 1$, and it then
135: reads $M \mid G(N)$ exactly when $M \mid (m+1)^2$, $2M \mid P - m^2$ and
136: $2M \mid R - (m+2)^2$. Both hypotheses hold here: the three factors are prime,
137: and the Korselt divisibilities hold by \cref{lem:least-safe-consec-korselt} at $m = 209$,
138: $K = 23601123000$: $2K+3 = 209 \cdot 225848067$, $K = 210 \cdot 112386300$ and
139: $2K+2m+1 = 211 \cdot 223707329$. So, reading the closed form, the divisors of
140: $G(N)$ are the common divisors of $(m+1)^2 = 44100$,
141: $(P - m^2)/2 = 2466317331660$ and $(R - (m+2)^2)/2 = 2489918454240$,
142: whose gcd is $420$; hence $G(N) \mid 420$, and $420 \mid G(N)$ because the form
143: reads the three divisibilities at $M = 420$. So $G(N) = 420$, as the row's value
144: theorem \lean{residueGcd\_consec\_record\_eq\_four\_twenty} records.
145: 
146: The member is composite. Were $N$ prime, its invariant would be $0$
147: (\cref{thm:least-safe-zero-iff-prime}, \lean{residueGcd\_eq\_zero\_iff\_prime}), against
148: $G(N) = 420$. So $G(N) = 420 \ge 1$ makes $\ell(N)$ defined at this value, and
149: $\ell(N)$ is the least non-divisor of $420$: each of $2, \ldots, 7$ divides
150: $420 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7$, and $8$ does not, so $\ell(N) = 8$, as the
151: row's value theorem \lean{leastSafeModulus\_four\_twenty} records. The second
152: row's member is therefore an odd composite with $\ell(N) = 8$.
153: \end{proof}
