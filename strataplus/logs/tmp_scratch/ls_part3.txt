34: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{exists\_leastSafeModulus\_eq\_seventeen}
35: \label{thm:least-safe-exists-seventeen}
36: There is an odd composite $N$ with $\ell(N) = 17$.
37: \end{theorem}
38: \begin{proof}
39: Take the fifth row of \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder}: the member $N$ of the
40: consecutive family (\cite{Companion-monograph}) at $m = 360359$ and
41: $K = 28475431726698356400$, whose three factors are
42: \[
43:   \begin{gathered}
44:     P = 10261378101601293013947601, \\
45:     Q = 10261406577033019712304001, \\
46:     R = 10261435052464746410660401 .
47:   \end{gathered}
48: \]
49: 
50: The three factors are prime. Each of them carries a certificate, and the checker
51: accepts all three (\lean{prattOK\_lsm17\_P}, \lean{prattOK\_lsm17\_Q},
52: \lean{prattOK\_lsm17\_R}); by \cref{thm:least-safe-pratt-sound} the number each
53: certificate names is prime (\lean{prime\_lsm17\_P}, \lean{prime\_lsm17\_Q},
54: \lean{prime\_lsm17\_R}).
55: 
56: The invariant of the member is the value stated for the record. The closed form
57: of \cref{thm:least-safe-consec-closed-form}
58: (\lean{consec\_dvd\_residueGcd\_iff}) reads, on a member of the family whose
59: three factors are prime, $M \mid G(N)$ exactly when $M \mid (m+1)^2$,
60: $2M \mid P - m^2$ and $2M \mid R - (m+2)^2$. Both hypotheses hold here: the three
61: factors are prime, as just shown, and the Korselt divisibilities $P - 1 = mK$,
62: $Q - 1 = (m+1)K$ and $R - 1 = (m+2)K$ divide $N - 1$, being part of the family's
63: data (\cite{Companion-monograph}). The divisors of $G(N)$ are therefore the
64: divisors of the gcd of $(m+1)^2$, $(P - m^2)/2$ and $(R - (m+2)^2)/2$, and that
65: gcd is $G(N) = 6486480$, as the value theorem
66: \lean{residueGcd\_consec\_record\_eq\_six\_four\_eight\_six\_four\_eight\_zero}
67: records.
68: 
69: The member is composite. Were $N$ prime, its invariant would be $0$
70: (\cref{thm:least-safe-zero-iff-prime}, \lean{residueGcd\_eq\_zero\_iff\_prime}),
71: against the value just read; so $G(N) = 6486480$ is positive, which is what makes
72: $\ell(N)$ defined at that value, and $\ell(N)$ is the least non-divisor of
73: $6486480$. Each of $2, \ldots, 16$ divides $6486480$ and $17$ does not, so
74: $\ell(N) = 17$, as the value theorem
75: \lean{leastSafeModulus\_six\_four\_eight\_six\_four\_eight\_zero} records.
76: \end{proof}

1: \section{The conditional lower bound}
2: \label{sec:least-safe-conditional_ladder}
3: 
4: \Cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-le} bounded $\ell(N)$ above, with no hypothesis
5: anywhere, and every value of $\ell$ that the two preceding sections exhibited
6: sits far below that bound at its own $N$. The question that is left is the other
7: direction: whether, for every $B$, some $N$ has all of $1, 2, \ldots, B$ among the divisors of
8: $G(N)$. Nothing in this paper settles that question without a hypothesis. By
9: \cref{thm:least-safe-lt-of-icc-dvd} the forcing is the single divisibility
10: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \mid G(N)$, and this section shows that the
11: divisibility holds at the consecutive class at $m = 2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) - 1$:
12: an admissible $K$ there (one satisfying the floor $m + 3 \le K$ and the congruences of the class,
13: with $mK+1$, $(m+1)K+1$ and $(m+2)K+1$ prime)
14: yields a composite $N$ with $\ell(N) > B$, and a bound on the size of $K$ gives $\log_2 N$ at
15: most a constant times $B$. The hypothesis is that such a $K$ exists, with that size bound;
16: everything else below is elementary, including the one estimate that the sharpest
17: form of the bound spends, $\log_2 \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \le 4B$, which is
18: proved here rather than assumed. The hypothesis is stated once, and every result
19: that consumes it names it in its header.
20: 
21: The mechanism of that construction is Erdős's, and it is worth naming as such.
22: Instead of asking which primes $p$ satisfy $p - 1 \mid N - 1$, it fixes the conductor
23: $L$ first, takes primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod{L}$ and looks for a subset product
24: $\equiv 1 \pmod{L}$: this is the device described in \S1 and carried out in \S3 of
25: \cite{GranvillePomerance2002}, and at a single conductor the three forms are those of
26: \cite{Chernick1939} at $m = 1$. What the hypothesis below is spent on is the reading
27: $\ell(N) > B$, that is $L \mid G(N)$ in place of $L \mid N - 1$, together with the size
28: bound on $K$.
29: 
30: \begin{definition}\lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize}
31: \label{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size}
32: Let $C \ge 1$, and let $B \ge 2$. Write $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$ and
33: $m = 2L - 1$. The size hypothesis \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize}\ $C$ asks that at
34: this conductor there be an integer $K$ which satisfies the size bound (the first
35: inequality below) and is \emph{admissible}, that is, satisfies the floor and the congruences
36: \[
37: \begin{gathered}
38:   K \le \bigl(m(m+1)(m+2)\bigr)^C, \qquad m + 3 \le K, \\
39:   (m+1) \mid K, \qquad m \mid 2K + 3, \qquad (m+2) \mid 2K + 2m + 1,
40: \end{gathered}
41: \]
42: and that its three factors $P = mK + 1$, $Q = (m+1)K + 1$ and $R = (m+2)K + 1$ be
43: prime. It does so for every $B \ge 2$.
44: \end{definition}
45: 
46: The bound in that hypothesis is a power of the crude conductor of the Korselt
47: class at $m$, the product $m(m+1)(m+2)$ (\lean{consecAtStep}); the size-free part
48: of an admissible $K$, which is the form the core lemma consumes, is
49: \lean{ConsecAdm}, and \lean{DicksonConsecAtSize} is the same list of conditions
50: with the bound attached at a general conductor $m$. The choice $m = 2L - 1$ is
51: the one that matters here, because it makes $m + 1 = 2L$, so the congruence
52: $(m+1) \mid K$ becomes $2L \mid K$: the divisibility by half the class that the
53: invariant takes below. Nothing in this paper proves \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize};
54: its two consequences here are marked conditional in their headers.
55: 
56: The construction rests on a size-free core.
57: 
58: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{exists\_blind\_core}
59: \label{lem:least-safe-exists-blind-core}
60: Let $L \ge 2$ and let $K$ be admissible at $m = 2L - 1$. Then there is an odd
61: composite $N$ with $G(N) \ge 1$, with $L \mid G(N)$, and with
62: $N = PQR$ at the three factors of $K$ at this $m$.
63: \end{lemma}
64: \begin{proof}
65: Unpack the admissibility: the floor $m + 3 \le K$, the three congruences
66: $(m+1) \mid K$, $m \mid 2K + 3$ and $(m+2) \mid 2K + 2m + 1$, and the primality of
67: $P = mK + 1$, $Q = (m+1)K + 1$ and $R = (m+2)K + 1$.
68: 
69: Those three congruences are exactly Korselt's condition on the consecutive
70: family (\cref{lem:least-safe-consec-korselt}, \lean{consec\_korselt}): they are what makes
71: each of $P - 1 = mK$, $Q - 1 = (m+1)K$ and $R - 1 = (m+2)K$ divide
72: $N - 1 = PQR - 1$.
73: 
74: The product $N = PQR$ is odd, and at least $3$. Here $m = 2L - 1$ is odd while
75: $m + 1 = 2L$ is even, and $(m+1) \mid K$ then forces $K$ to be even; with $m$ odd
76: and $K$ even each of the three factors is odd, and a product of odd integers is
77: odd. Each factor is greater than $1$, so the product is at least $3$; the members
78: of the family are the odd integers at least $3$, so the value $PQR$ is the member
79: $N$ of the statement, and it is the member the divisibilities above are about.
80: 
81: The member is composite. Its product form is a product of two integers greater
82: than $1$, namely $P \cdot Q$ and $R$, and a product of two integers greater than
83: $1$ is not prime.
84: 
85: It remains to read $L \mid G(N)$ off the family. The half-class part of the
86: invariant's closed form on the consecutive family
87: (\cref{lem:least-safe-consec-half}, \lean{consec\_dvd\_residueGcd\_half}) says that a
88: member at $m = 2M - 1$ has $M \mid G(N)$ as soon as its three factors are prime,
89: the three Korselt divisibilities $mK \mid N - 1$, $(m+1)K \mid N - 1$ and
90: $(m+2)K \mid N - 1$ hold, $2M \mid K$, and $K \ge 2M + 2$. At $M = L$ every hypothesis is in hand:
91: $m = 2L - 1$; the three factors are prime; the three divisibilities are the ones
92: obtained above; $2L \mid K$ because $m + 1 = 2L$ divides $K$; and $K \ge m + 3 = 2L + 2$, the floor of the admissibility. Explicitly, with
93: $K = 2L \cdot K'$ the divisibilities it asks for read
94: \[
95: \begin{gathered}
96:   L \mid (m+1)^2 = 4L^2, \qquad
97:   2L \mid P - m^2 = 2L\,(mK' - m + 1), \\
98:   2L \mid R - (m+2)^2 = 2L\,((m+2)K' - (m+3)),
99: \end{gathered}
100: \]
101: each visibly satisfied. Hence $L \mid G(N)$.
102: 
103: Finally $G(N) \ge 1$: the invariant is $0$ exactly at the primes
104: (\cref{thm:least-safe-zero-iff-prime}, \lean{residueGcd\_eq\_zero\_iff\_prime}), and $N$ is
105: composite, so it is not $0$. The oddness and the compositeness just established,
106: with that positivity, the divisibility $L \mid G(N)$ and the identity
107: $N = PQR$, are the statement.
108: \end{proof}
109: 
110: The size form is one line further, and it is the shape both bounds below consume.
111: 
112: \begin{proposition}\lvCond\lean{exists\_blind\_of\_lcm\_size}
113: \label{prop:least-safe-exists-blind-lcm-size}
114: Let $C \ge 1$ and $L \ge 2$, and assume the size hypothesis
115: \lean{DicksonConsecAtSize}\ $C$ at the conductor $m = 2L - 1$: an admissible $K$
116: at $m$ with $K \le \bigl(m(m+1)(m+2)\bigr)^C$ exists. Then there is an odd
117: composite $N$ with
118: \[
119:   L \mid G(N), \qquad
120:   \log_2 N \;\le\; 10 + 21C + (3 + 9C)\log_2 L .
121: \]
122: \end{proposition}
123: \begin{proof}
124: The hypothesis is the admissibility of the core lemma together with the bound on
125: $K$, so an admissible $K$ at $m = 2L - 1$ with $K \le \operatorname{consecAtStep}(m)^C$
126: is available, and \cref{lem:least-safe-exists-blind-core} applies: there is an odd
127: composite $N$ with $L \mid G(N)$ and $N = PQR$.
128: 
129: For the size, the three factors are bounded by the conductor and $K$ together:
130: $PQR \le 2(m+2)^3K^3$ (\lean{consec\_at\_N\_le}). Here $m + 2 = 2L + 1 \le 4L$, and
131: $\operatorname{consecAtStep}(2L-1) \le 16L^3$
132: (\lean{consecAtStep\_le\_of\_lcm}), so $K \le (16L^3)^C$ and each factor of the
133: product is below a power of two built from $L$ and $C$; multiplying the bounds and
134: taking $\log_2$ gives
135: \[
136:   \log_2 N \;\le\; 10 + 21C + (3 + 9C)\log_2 L
137: \]
138: (\lean{log2\_N\_le\_of\_lcm\_size}), which is the displayed bound read at the
139: identity $N = PQR$ above; those constants are the formal statement's and are not optimised.
140: \end{proof}

142: \begin{theorem}\lvCond\lean{leastSafeModulus\_gt\_conditional}
143: \label{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-conditional}
144: Assume the size hypothesis \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize}\ $C$ of
145: \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size}, for some $C \ge 1$. Then for
146: every $B \ge 2$ there is an odd composite $N$ with
147: \[
148:   \ell(N) > B, \qquad
149:   \log_2 N \;\le\; (13 + 30C)\,B\,(\log_2 B + 1).
150: \]
151: \end{theorem}
152: \begin{proof}
153: Fix $B \ge 2$ and put $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$. Then $L \ge 2$: the
154: integer $2$ lies in $[1,B]$ for $B \ge 2$, so it divides $L$, and $L$ is not $0$
155: (\lean{two\_le\_lcm\_Icc}). This is the criterion's hypothesis made concrete ---
156: the bound about to be forced is the least common multiple itself.
157: 
158: The size hypothesis is used here for the first time, at the conductor this $B$
159: determines: \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize}\ $C$ is, at $B$, the statement that an
160: admissible $K$ with $K \le \operatorname{consecAtStep}(2L-1)^C$ exists.
161: 
162: By \cref{prop:least-safe-exists-blind-lcm-size} there is an odd composite $N$
163: with $L \mid G(N)$ and $\log_2 N \le 10 + 21C + (3+9C)\log_2 L$.
164: 
165: Every $m$ with $1 \le m \le B$ divides $L$ --- that is the defining property of a
166: least common multiple --- hence divides $G(N)$ by transitivity. So
167: \cref{thm:least-safe-lt-of-icc-dvd} (\lean{lt\_leastSafeModulus\_of\_Icc\_dvd})
168: applies and gives $B < \ell(N)$. The construction forces
169: the one divisibility that the criterion reads as the absence of a witness below $B$.
170: 
171: It remains to bound $\log_2 L$ by $B$ alone. The least common multiple of
172: $1, \ldots, B$ divides $B!$ (\lean{lcm\_Icc\_dvd\_factorial}), and $B!$ is at most
173: $B^B$ for every $B$ (\cite{Mathlib2020}, \lean{Nat.factorial\_le\_pow}); so $L \le B^B$, which
174: is the same pair of facts read at $L$ (\lean{lcm\_Icc\_le\_factorial}). As
175: $B < 2^{\log_2 B + 1}$, raising to the $B$-th power gives
176: $B^B < 2^{B(\log_2 B + 1)}$, hence
177: $\log_2 L \le B(\log_2 B + 1)$ (\lean{log2\_lcm\_Icc\_lt},
178: \lean{log2\_lcm\_Icc\_le\_mul}). The two exponents of this section are named here:
179: the factorial form above carries the exponent \lean{lsmFacExponent}, which at the
180: hypothesis's constant $C$ is $13 + 30C$, and the linear form stated below carries
181: \lean{lsmLinExponent}, the same $13 + 30C$ scaled by the comparison of $4$ against $\log_2 B + 1$. The named class
182: \lean{LcmLogBound}\ $\kappa$ is the statement
183: $\log_2 \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \le \kappa B$ at every $B \ge 1$, and its
184: $\kappa = 4$ case is discharged a few paragraphs on.
185: 
186: The constants absorb. Since $B(\log_2 B + 1) \ge 1$, the term
187: $10 + 21C$ is at most $(10+21C) \cdot B(\log_2 B + 1)$, and adding
188: $(3+9C) \cdot B(\log_2 B + 1)$ to it gives
189: $(13 + 30C) \cdot B(\log_2 B + 1)$. So there is an odd composite $N$ with
190: $\ell(N) > B$ and $\log_2 N \le (13+30C)B(\log_2 B + 1)$.
191: \end{proof}

193: The linear form of that bound needs one estimate about the least common multiple
194: of an initial segment, and the estimate is proved here rather than assumed.

196: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{lcm\_Icc\_le\_four\_pow\_two\_mul}
197: \label{lem:least-safe-lcm-le-four-pow-two-mul}
198: For every $n \ge 0$, $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n) \le 4^{2n}$.
199: \end{lemma}
200: \begin{proof}
201: At $n = 0$ the empty least common multiple is $1$ and $4^0 = 1$.
202: 
203: For $n \ge 1$ the estimate is proved through the primorials, the products of the
204: primes below a bound. The argument has four parts, all machine-checked. First, the
205: least common multiple is the product of the primorials at the integer roots:
206: \[
207:   \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n) \;=\; \prod_{k \le \log_2 n}
208:     \operatorname{primorial}\bigl(\lfloor n^{1/k}\rfloor\bigr)
209:   \qquad (\lean{lcm\_Icc\_eq\_prod\_primorial}).
210: \]
211: Second, that identity is prime by prime. At a prime $p$ the exponent of $p$ in
212: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)$ is $\log_p n$, which counts the $k$ with
213: $p^k \le n$ (\lean{factorization\_lcm\_Icc}, \lean{log\_eq\_card\_pow\_le}), and
214: those $k$ all lie below $\log_2 n$, since $\log_p n \le \log_2 n$
215: (\lean{log\_le\_log2}); on the right, the exponent of $p$ in the product counts
216: the $k$ with $p \le \lfloor n^{1/k}\rfloor$ (\lean{factorization\_primorial}, with
217: \lean{factorization\_finset\_lcm} and \lean{factorization\_le\_log} supplying the
218: factorization of a least common multiple and the bound on one exponent of it), and
219: $p \le n^{1/k}$ is the same condition as $p^k \le n$. Third, each primorial is at
220: most $4$ raised to its argument (\cite{Mathlib2020}, \lean{primorial\_le\_4\_pow}), so
221: the least common multiple is at most
222: $4^{\sum_{k \le \log_2 n} \lfloor n^{1/k}\rfloor}$. Fourth, that root sum is at
223: most $2n$: the term at $k = 1$ is $n$ itself, and each of the terms from $k = 2$ on
224: is at most $\sqrt n$ (\lean{nthRoot\_le\_sqrt}), while there are at most
225: $\sqrt n$ of them, because the indices run over $2, \ldots, \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor$ and
226: $\log_2 n - 1 \le \sqrt n$
227: (\lean{sum\_nthRoot\_le\_two\_mul}, \lean{log2\_le\_sqrt\_succ}, the last from
228: $(s+1)^2 < 2^{s+2}$, \lean{sq\_succ\_lt\_two\_pow}).
229: 
230: Hence $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n) \le 4^{2n}$ for every $n$, and no
231: hypothesis was used anywhere in the argument.
232: \end{proof}

234: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{lcmLogBound\_four}
235: \label{thm:least-safe-lcm-log-bound-four}
236: For every $B \ge 1$,
237: $\log_2 \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \le 4B$: the hypothesis class
238: \lean{LcmLogBound} holds at $\kappa = 4$.
239: \end{theorem}
240: \begin{proof}
241: In base two, \cref{lem:least-safe-lcm-le-four-pow-two-mul} reads
242: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \le 4^{2B} = 2^{4B}$
243: (\lean{lcm\_Icc\_le\_two\_pow\_four\_mul}, the same estimate with the base
244: changed). A power-of-two bound inverts into a logarithmic one: $n \le 2^k$ gives
245: $\log_2 n \le k$ (\lean{log2\_le\_of\_le\_pow}). At $k = 4B$ and
246: $n = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$ the two give
247: $\log_2 \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \le 4B$ for every $B \ge 1$. So the
248: $\kappa = 4$ case of the named class is a theorem, proved from the estimate alone,
249: and the bound below spends nothing beyond it.
250: \end{proof}

252: \begin{theorem}\lvCond\lean{leastSafeModulus\_gt\_linear\_conditional}
253: \label{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-linear-conditional}
254: Assume the size hypothesis \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize}\ $C$ of
255: \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size}, for some $C \ge 1$. Then for
256: every $B \ge 2$ there is an odd composite $N$ with
257: \[
258:   \ell(N) > B, \qquad \log_2 N \;\le\; (52 + 120C)\,B .
259: \]
260: \end{theorem}
261: \begin{proof}
262: Fix $B \ge 2$ and put $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$, so that $L \ge 2$
263: (\lean{two\_le\_lcm\_Icc}). The size hypothesis at this $B$ gives an admissible $K$
264: at $m = 2L - 1$ with $K \le \operatorname{consecAtStep}(2L-1)^C$, and
265: \cref{prop:least-safe-exists-blind-lcm-size} yields an odd composite $N$ with
266: $L \mid G(N)$ and $\log_2 N \le 10 + 21C + (3+9C)\log_2 L$.
267: 
268: Every $m$ with $1 \le m \le B$ divides $L$, hence $G(N)$, so
269: \cref{thm:least-safe-lt-of-icc-dvd} (\lean{lt\_leastSafeModulus\_of\_Icc\_dvd})
270: gives $B < \ell(N)$, exactly as at the factorial form above; only the bound on
271: $\log_2 N$ changes.
272: 
273: The discharge of \cref{thm:least-safe-lcm-log-bound-four} replaces the factorial
274: bound: $\log_2 L \le 4B$, so
275: $\log_2 N \le 10 + 21C + (3+9C) \cdot 4B$. The constants absorb again, now against
276: a multiple of $B$: $10 + 21C \le (10+21C)B$ because $B \ge 2$, and the two terms
277: add to $10 + 21C + (12 + 36C)B \le (52 + 120C)B$, which is
278: \lean{lsmLinExponent}\ $C \cdot B$, that exponent's value being $52 + 120C$.
279: 
280: So for every $B \ge 2$ there is an odd composite $N$ with $\ell(N) > B$ and
281: $\log_2 N \le (52+120C)B$.
282: \end{proof}
283: 
284: The two bounds can now be compared. \Cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-le} sees every odd
285: composite by a modulus of size at most $2\log_2(N(N-1)) + 3$, with no hypothesis;
286: \cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-linear-conditional} exhibits, under
287: \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize}\ $C$ and only under it, composites whose least safe
288: modulus exceeds $B$ while $\log_2 N$ stays below a constant multiple of $B$.
289: Along those members and under that hypothesis, $\ell$ and $\log N$ grow at the
290: same rate; neither inequality alone says anything about the growth of $\ell$ at an
291: arbitrary composite. The two constants are related: the linear constant $52 + 120C$ is $4 \cdot (13 + 30C)$, so the two
292: forms differ only in the factor $4B$ of the linear form against $B(\log_2 B + 1)$ of the
293: factorial form. With $\log_2$ the integer part, $\log_2 B + 1 > 4$ exactly when $B \ge 16$, so
294: the linear form is the sharper of the two from $B = 16$ on, at every $C$.
295: 
296: The smallest bound the records exhibit makes the shape of those inequalities
297: visible by hand. At the second row of \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder} the invariant
298: is $420$, each of $2, \ldots, 7$ divides it and $8$ does not, and the member has
299: $2^{126} \le N < 2^{127}$; the criterion's divisibility holds at $B = 7$, where
300: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,7) = 420$ divides $420$, and fails at $B = 8$, where
301: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,8) = 840$ does not. So $B = 7$ is the largest bound
302: at which the divisibility holds at that row, its $\ell$ is $B + 1$, and its
303: $\log_2 N$ is $126$, far below the linear bound
304: $(52 + 120C) \cdot 7 = 1204$ at $C = 1$ (the factorial form allows $(13 + 30) \cdot 7 \cdot (\log_2 7 + 1) = 43 \cdot 7 \cdot 3 = 903$ there).
305: The row is not the construction's member: the member the theorem builds sits at
306: $m = 2 \cdot 420 - 1 = 839$, the row at $m = 209$. The hypothesis is not
307: discharged by any row.
308: 
309: One modulus at a time, and under a hypothesis at that single modulus, the same construction says that
310: no fixed modulus has a witness at every odd composite.

312: \begin{theorem}\lvCond\lean{constant\_modulus\_fails\_size\_conditional}
313: \label{thm:least-safe-constant-modulus-fails}
314: Let $C \ge 1$ and let $M \ge 2$ be a modulus, and assume the size hypothesis
315: \lean{DicksonConsecAtSizeMod}\ $C$ $M$: at the conductor $m = 2M - 1$ there is an
316: admissible $K$ with $K \le \bigl(m(m+1)(m+2)\bigr)^C$. Then there is an odd
317: composite $N$ such that no base makes $M$ a witness for $N$, with
318: \[
319:   \log_2 N \;\le\; 10 + 21C + (3 + 9C)\log_2 M .
320: \]
321: \end{theorem}
322: \begin{proof}
323: By its definition \lean{DicksonConsecAtSizeMod}\ $C$ $M$ is the size hypothesis
324: at the conductor $2M - 1$, that is, it is
325: \lean{DicksonConsecAtSize}\ $C$ $(2M-1)$: the same construction as the two bounds
326: above, with $M$ in place of $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$.
327: 
328: So \cref{prop:least-safe-exists-blind-lcm-size} applies at $L = M$, and there is
329: an odd composite $N$ with $M \mid G(N)$ and
330: $\log_2 N \le 10 + 21C + (3+9C)\log_2 M$.
331: 
332: The divisibility is the absence of a witness: by \cref{thm:least-safe-criterion}
333: (\lean{noWitnessMod\_iff\_dvd\_residueGcd}) a modulus $M$ divides $G(N)$ exactly
334: when no base makes it a witness, so $M \mid G(N)$ is the statement that $N$ is a
335: $C_M$-pseudoprime (\cref{def:least-safe-pseudoprime}). That, with the bound, is
336: the theorem.
337: \end{proof}
338: 
339: \Cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} is the only unproved input of the
340: two bounds of this section. The core \cref{lem:least-safe-exists-blind-core},
341: the estimate \cref{lem:least-safe-lcm-le-four-pow-two-mul} and its discharge
342: \cref{thm:least-safe-lcm-log-bound-four} carry no hypothesis at all, so the linear
343: form uses no hypothesis besides the one in its header.
344: 
345: The Lean development also contains a second form of the construction, in the same file as the
346: bounds above; the paragraph records it only to locate its names, and nothing in the paper uses
347: it. It rests on a different hypothesis, which is not a consequence of the first. There the
348: conductor is the crude one $\lean{consecStep}\ t$ at the power of two $m = 2^t - 1$
349: rather than at a least common multiple, and it stays below $2^{4t+1}$
350: (\lean{consecStep\_lt\_pow}); the size hypothesis is \lean{DicksonConsecSize}\ $C$,
351: which asks for an admissible $K$ of size at most $\lean{consecStep}\ t$ raised to
352: $C$ at every $t \ge 1$. What
353: follows from it is the same reading of the one number, at the modulus $2^{2t}$
354: and with the rate expressed through the denominator
355: \lean{sizeExponent}\ $C = 2 + 8C$: the doubling
356: $2 \cdot 2^t K = 2^{t+1}K$ (\lean{two\_mul\_pow\_eq}), the bounds on the three
357: factors (\lean{consec\_factor\_le}) and on their product
358: (\lean{consec\_N\_le}), the logarithmic form
359: $\log_2 N \le (3 + 12C)t + 3C + 2$ under $K \le \lean{consecStep}\ t ^ C$
360: (\lean{log2\_N\_le\_of\_size}), and the arithmetic that
361: $(2 + 8C) \cdot 2t$ dominates that expression
362: (\lean{sizeExponent\_covers\_bound}). Its two conditional conclusions, which are the same statement with the exponent
363: read off, are in the Lean development: a $C_{2^{2t}}$-pseudoprime $N$ with $\log_2 N$ at most
364: $\lean{sizeExponent}\ C \cdot 2t$ (\lean{ladder\_lower\_size\_conditional}), and
365: the same data read as $2t \le v_2(G(N))$ together with that size bound
366: (\lean{j\_ge\_c\_log\_conditional}). The bounds above never use this second
367: packaging, and their conclusions are of the form $B < \ell(N)$ and not of the form
368: $2t \le v_2(G(N))$.
