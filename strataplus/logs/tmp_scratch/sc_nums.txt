27:  r \;=\; (m - 1)^{N-1} \bmod N \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\} \qquad (\lean{resid}),

34:The base $m$ runs over all of $[1,N]$, invertible modulo $N$ or not. The residue $0$ is left out because it contributes the term $0$ to the gcd and so constrains no modulus. A base $b$ is a \emph{witness at} $M$ when $r \ne 0$ and $M \nmid r - 1$; the modulus $M$ \emph{has no witness at} $N$ when no base is one. The reduction modulo $M$ of the Fermat test is written $C_M$, and a base that is a witness at $M$ is a \emph{$C_M$-witness}. An odd composite $N$ such that no base $m \in [1,N]$ is a $C_M$-witness --- equivalently, such that $M \mid G(N)$ --- is a \emph{$C_M$-pseudoprime}; a composite at which some modulus $M \ge 2$ has no witness is \emph{exceptional}, equivalently $G(N) > 1$.

51:In the gcd defining $G(N)$, the base $m$ contributes the term $r - 1$ when its residue is nonzero and the term $0$ when that residue is $0$.
