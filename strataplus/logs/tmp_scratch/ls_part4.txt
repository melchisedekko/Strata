1: \section{Where it bites}
2: \label{sec:least-safe-where_it_bites}
3: 
4: Every conditional statement of \cref{sec:least-safe-conditional_ladder} rests on
5: one number: at the conductor $m = 2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) - 1$ there is an
6: admissible multiplier $K$, bounded in size
7: (\cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size}). Its demands on $K$ are of two
8: kinds. The first is congruence, three conditions,
9: \[
10:   (m+1) \mid K, \qquad m \mid 2K + 3, \qquad (m+2) \mid 2K + 2m + 1 .
11: \]
12: They are conditions on the residue of $K$ modulo the product $m(m+1)(m+2)$
13: alone, so a bounded quantity decides them, and if the class they describe has a
14: member at all then it has one below that product --- at no cost in hypothesis. The
15: second is the primality of the three numbers $mK+1$, $(m+1)K+1$ and $(m+2)K+1$,
16: which is not a divisibility of $K$ at all. Every conditional statement of this
17: paper assumes the primality of those three numbers, in one form or another; none
18: proves it. The rest of the
19: section asks how little of the size side the rate needs.
20: 
21: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{consecDiv\_iff\_mod}
22: \label{thm:least-safe-consec-div-iff-mod}
23: Let $m$ and $K$ be natural numbers and put $S = m(m+1)(m+2)$; let
24: \lean{ConsecDiv} $m$ $K$ be the conjunction of the three divisibilities
25: \[
26:   (m+1) \mid K, \qquad m \mid 2K + 3, \qquad (m+2) \mid 2K + 2m + 1 .
27: \]
28: Then \lean{ConsecDiv} $m$ $K$ holds if and only if \lean{ConsecDiv} $m$
29: $(K \bmod S)$ holds.
30: \end{theorem}
31: \begin{proof}
32: Those three divisibilities are the Korselt conditions on the multiplier of the
33: consecutive family (\cite{Companion-monograph}), and the theorem says that they
34: see the residue of $K$ modulo the product of their three moduli and nothing
35: else. What makes $S$ a period is that it satisfies each of the three conditions
36: with the coefficient that condition carries: $(m+1) \mid S$, since
37: $S = (m+1) \cdot m(m+2)$; then $m \mid 2S$, since $2S = m \cdot 2(m+1)(m+2)$; and
38: $(m+2) \mid 2S$, since $2S = (m+2) \cdot 2m(m+1)$. Adding $S$ times an integer to
39: $K$ therefore moves none of the three conditions, and that two-sided
40: equivalence, at an arbitrary pair of a residue and a multiplier of the period,
41: is \lean{consecDiv\_add\_mul}.
42: 
43: Every natural number leaves a remainder and a quotient on division by $S$, and
44: $K$ is the sum of the remainder $K \bmod S$ and a multiple of $S$. By the
45: previous paragraph the three divisibilities hold at $K$ exactly when they hold
46: at that remainder, so reading them at $K \bmod S$ in place of $K$ is lossless,
47: which is the statement.
48: \end{proof}
49: 
50: Deciding the three congruences is therefore a bounded computation, and the next
51: statement is its consequence: the multipliers satisfying them are decided by a
52: residue below the period.
53: 
54: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{exists\_residue\_lt\_step}
55: \label{lem:least-safe-exists-residue-lt-step}
56: Let $m \ge 1$ and let $K$ be admissible at $m$: let it satisfy $m + 3 \le K$, let
57: the three divisibilities of \cref{thm:least-safe-consec-div-iff-mod} hold of it,
58: and let the three numbers $mK+1$, $(m+1)K+1$ and $(m+2)K+1$ be prime. Then there
59: is a natural number $r$ with $r < m(m+1)(m+2)$ and with \lean{ConsecDiv} $m$ $r$.
60: \end{lemma}
61: \begin{proof}
62: Unpack the admissibility: the floor $m + 3 \le K$, the three divisibilities, and
63: the primality of $P = mK+1$, $Q = (m+1)K+1$ and $R = (m+2)K+1$. The proof keeps
64: the three divisibilities and discards the floor and the three primalities ---
65: that discard is the content of the lemma --- and the step that keeps them is
66: \lean{ConsecAdm.toDiv}, which reads the admissibility as the conjunction
67: \lean{ConsecDiv} $m$ $K$.
68: 
69: Let $r = K \bmod S$ with $S = m(m+1)(m+2)$. By
70: \cref{thm:least-safe-consec-div-iff-mod} the class holds at $r$, and $r < S$
71: because $S$ is the product of the three positive integers $m$, $m+1$ and $m+2$:
72: here, and here only, the hypothesis $1 \le m$ is used.
73: 
74: So a conductor at which the congruences have a solution at all has one below the
75: period. The lemma does not assert that they have a solution: what it cuts is the
76: size of a member of the class, and the existence of a member is the part of the
77: hypothesis that stays open.
78: \end{proof}
79: 
80: The smallest admissible pair this paper exhibits makes both halves visible. At
81: the first row of \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder} --- the member of
82: \cref{ex:least-safe-sixty}, whose invariant is $G(N) = 60$ --- the conductor is
83: $m = 29$ and the multiplier is $K = 6486240$, so the period of
84: \cref{thm:least-safe-consec-div-iff-mod} at that conductor is
85: $S = 29 \cdot 30 \cdot 31 = 26970$. The multiplier sits $240$ periods above a
86: residue of the period: $6486240 = 13440 + 240 \cdot 26970$, and $13440$ satisfies
87: all three divisibilities, $30 \mid 13440$, $29 \mid 2 \cdot 13440 + 3$ and
88: $31 \mid 2 \cdot 13440 + 2 \cdot 29 + 1$. At that residue the three numbers are
89: $29 \cdot 13440 + 1 = 389761$, which is prime --- trial division by the primes up
90: to $624$ decides it --- and $30 \cdot 13440 + 1 = 403201 = 191 \cdot 2111$ and
91: $31 \cdot 13440 + 1 = 416641 = 373 \cdot 1117$, two of the three composite. So a
92: residue below the period need not be admissible. The multiplier of the row need
93: not be the least admissible one at its conductor, and its conductor is not one of the
94: conductors $2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) - 1$ that
95: \cref{sec:least-safe-conditional_ladder} builds at: no bound $B$ gives $29$ of
96: that form, the values below it being $1$, $3$, $11$, $23$, and the next $119$.
97: 
98: The congruence half of the hypothesis costs no size:
99: \cref{thm:least-safe-consec-div-iff-mod} puts any solution of the congruences below the period
100: (for $m \ge 1$), and \cref{lem:least-safe-exists-residue-lt-step} applies this to an admissible $K$. The
101: primality of the three numbers $mK+1$, $(m+1)K+1$ and $(m+2)K+1$ is not proved in
102: this paper; every conditional statement of
103: \cref{sec:least-safe-conditional_ladder} and every conditional statement below
104: assumes it, in one form or another. In its qualitative form it is an instance of
105: Dickson's hypothesis on three linear forms at a single conductor, which is what
106: the hypothesis class of \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} says, and
107: all that it says. So the size hypothesis does not bite at the congruences:
108: whatever is asked of the admissible $K$, a polynomial bound on it or a
109: logarithmic one, what is not proved is the existence of the three primes.
110: 
111: A bound on the size of $K$ is more than the rate reads. What the proof of
112: \cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-conditional} used of the multiplier, in its bound on
113: $\log_2 N$, was its logarithm $\log_2 K$, and the hypothesis can be stated in that currency alone:
114: \lean{DicksonConsecAtLog} takes an exponent $E$ and a conductor and asks for one
115: admissible $K$ there with $\log_2 K \le E$, while
116: \lean{DicksonConsecLcmLog} is the same requirement at the conductor
117: $m = 2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) - 1$ for every $B \ge 2$, the exponent read
118: as an arbitrary function $E$ of $B$. The requirement only becomes easier as the
119: exponent grows (\lean{DicksonConsecAtLog.mono}). The hypothesis of
120: \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} sits at the head of this family: a
121: bound $K \le \bigl(m(m+1)(m+2)\bigr)^C$ on the multiplier is the bound
122: $\log_2 K \le C\,(4 + 12B)$ on its logarithm (\lean{log2\_K\_le\_of\_lcm\_size}),
123: which is \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize.toLog}, and that in turn is the quadratic
124: form $\log_2 K \le 8CB^2$ (\lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize.toExp}), because
125: $4 + 12B \le 8B^2$ for $B \ge 2$. The converse is not claimed, so each form below
126: is a genuine weakening of the one before it.
127: 
128: \begin{theorem}\lvCond\lean{leastSafeModulus\_gt\_of\_log\_size}
129: \label{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-of-log-size}
130: Let $E$ and $B \ge 2$ be natural numbers, put $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$
131: and $m = 2L - 1$, and assume the hypothesis \lean{DicksonConsecAtLog} $E$ $m$ at
132: this one conductor: there is an admissible $K$ at $m$ with $\log_2 K \le E$.
133: Nothing is assumed at any other bound. Then there is an odd composite $N$ with
134: \[
135:   \ell(N) > B, \qquad \log_2 N \;\le\; 12B + 3E + 10 .
136: \]
137: \end{theorem}
138: \begin{proof}
139: Fix $B \ge 2$ and put $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$, $m = 2L - 1$. Then
140: $L \ge 2$: the integer $2$ lies in $\{1,\ldots,B\}$, so it divides $L$.
141: 
142: The hypothesis at this conductor is a pair, an admissible $K$ at $m$ together
143: with the bound $\log_2 K \le E$, and it is exactly what
144: \lean{exists\_blind\_of\_log\_size} consumes, the size-parametric form of the
145: core of \cref{lem:least-safe-exists-blind-core}. That theorem returns an odd
146: composite $N$ with $G(N) \ge 1$, with $L \mid G(N)$, and with
147: \[
148:   \log_2 N \;\le\; 3\log_2 L + 3E + 10 .
149: \]
150: The size bound inside it is the size of the product $N = PQR$ read through the
151: bounds on the conductor and on the multiplier: the product is at most
152: $2(m+2)^3K^3$, the bound proved in
153: \cref{sec:least-safe-conditional_ladder}, and taking logarithms of
154: $m + 2 < 2^{\log_2(m+2) + 1}$ and $K < 2^{\log_2 K + 1}$ gives
155: $\log_2 N \le 3\log_2(m+2) + 3E + 7$
156: (\lean{log2\_N\_le\_of\_pow\_bounds}, \lean{log2\_N\_le\_of\_log\_size}); at
157: $m = 2L - 1$ the factor $m + 2 = 2L + 1$ has
158: $\log_2(m+2) \le \log_2 L + 1$ (\lean{log2\_two\_mul\_add\_one\_le}), which is
159: how the displayed constant $3$ and the additive $10$ arise.
160: 
161: Every integer $j$ with $1 \le j \le B$ divides $L$, by the defining property of
162: a least common multiple, and hence divides $G(N)$ by transitivity. With
163: $G(N) \ge 1$ this is the criterion of \cref{thm:least-safe-lt-of-icc-dvd} at the
164: bound $B$ (\lean{lt\_leastSafeModulus\_of\_Icc\_dvd}), which reads the
165: divisibilities below $B$ as the single inequality $B < \ell(N)$. The same step
166: was taken by the bounds of \cref{sec:least-safe-conditional_ladder}, and only
167: the bound on $\log_2 N$ changes here.
168: 
169: It remains to spend the estimate on the least common multiple that
170: \cref{thm:least-safe-lcm-log-bound-four} proves without hypothesis:
171: $\log_2 L \le 4B$ for every $B \ge 1$. Composing it with the logarithmic bound
172: on $N$ gives $\log_2 N \le 3 \cdot 4B + 3E + 10 = 12B + 3E + 10$, the displayed
173: inequality.
174: \end{proof}
175: 
176: One bound $B$ at a time: the hypothesis is consumed at a single conductor and
177: produces a single composite, so a statement about how often $\ell$ exceeds $B$
178: needs the hypothesis only at the bounds that statement names. That is why the
179: statement above is the master form of the size hypothesis, and why what the rate
180: reads is the exponent, and not the size of $K$.
181: 
182: \begin{theorem}\lvCond\lean{leastSafeModulus\_gt\_exp\_conditional}
183: \label{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-exp-conditional}
184: Let $A$ be a natural number and assume the hypothesis \lean{DicksonConsecLcmExp}
185: $A$: for every $B \ge 2$, at the conductor $m = 2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) - 1$
186: there is an admissible $K$ with $\log_2 K \le A B^2$. Then for every $B \ge 2$
187: there is an odd composite $N$ with
188: \[
189:   \ell(N) > B, \qquad \log_2 N \;\le\; (3A + 9)B^2 .
190: \]
191: \end{theorem}
192: \begin{proof}
193: Fix $B \ge 2$ and put $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$. The hypothesis at
194: this $B$ is the hypothesis of \cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-of-log-size} at the
195: exponent $E = AB^2$, so that theorem produces an odd composite $N$ with
196: $\ell(N) > B$ and $\log_2 N \le 12B + 3AB^2 + 10$.
197: 
198: Two of those terms are absorbed by the quadratic growth of the exponent. For
199: $B \ge 2$ the difference $9B^2 - 12B - 10$ is increasing and equals $2$ at
200: $B = 2$, so $12B + 10 \le 9B^2$ from $B = 2$ on. Hence
201: $12B + 3AB^2 + 10 \le (3A + 9)B^2$, which is the displayed bound.
202: \end{proof}
203: 
204: The rate that this buys is a square root. For each member the theorem produces
205: at $B$, the inequality $B < \ell(N)$ and the bound
206: $\log_2 N \le (3A + 9)B^2$ combine into
207: $\ell(N) > \sqrt{\log_2 N / (3A + 9)}$: quasi-polynomial size in the multiplier
208: already forces the least safe modulus of those members to grow like the square
209: root of the logarithm of the number. The conclusion is weaker than the linear
210: rate that \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} gives, and so is the
211: hypothesis, in one direction: a polynomial bound on the multiplier gives the
212: linear exponent in $B$ (\lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize.toLog}) and hence the
213: quadratic one (\lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize.toExp}). Whether the quadratic hypothesis is strictly weaker is not known. The square root is that pair read at the members the
214: theorem produces, with the hypothesis named in the same sentence; no statement
215: of this paper asserts such a rate for the composites at large.
216: 
217: \begin{theorem}\lvCond\lean{leastSafeModulus\_gt\_of\_dickson\_linnik}
218: \label{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-of-dickson-linnik}
219: Let $E$ and $B \ge 2$ be natural numbers, put
220: $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$ and $m = 2L - 1$, and assume both halves of
221: the hypothesis at this conductor: that there be an admissible $K$ at $m$ above
222: every bound --- the qualitative Dickson hypothesis on the three linear forms,
223: \lean{DicksonConsecAt} $m$, which is the form the monograph uses for this family
224: (\cite{Companion-monograph}) --- and that, if an admissible $K$ at $m$ exists,
225: then one exists with $\log_2 K \le E$, that is, the first-hit bound
226: \lean{LinnikConsecAtLog} $E$ $m$. Then there is an odd composite $N$ with
227: \[
228:   \ell(N) > B, \qquad \log_2 N \;\le\; 12B + 3E + 10 .
229: \]
230: \end{theorem}
231: \begin{proof}
232: The two halves compose into the size form. The qualitative half produces an
233: admissible $K$ at $m$, since it produces one above every bound and the bound $0$
234: is one of them, and the quantitative half, applied to that existence, upgrades
235: it to an admissible $K$ with $\log_2 K \le E$. Their composition is
236: \lean{dicksonConsecAtLog\_of\_linnik}, and what it gives at this conductor is
237: nothing but the hypothesis of \cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-of-log-size} at
238: this $E$; that theorem then produces the odd composite, with the same bound
239: $12B + 3E + 10$ on $\log_2 N$.
240: \end{proof}
241: 
242: This is not a weakening of the hypothesis but a naming of its two halves, and
243: the two factors mean different things. The first is existence: an admissible
244: multiplier is there at all. The second is a first-hit bound: if there is one, one
245: is found soon. Existence alone bounds no size, so no statement about the rate can
246: rest on the first factor by itself. When the first-hit bound is linear in $B$ ---
247: the shape a polynomial bound on the multiplier takes in logarithms,
248: $\log_2 K \le C\,(4 + 12B)$, as \lean{DicksonConsecLcmSize.toLog} states it ---
249: the conclusion is again a bound linear in $B$, the shape of
250: \cref{thm:least-safe-modulus-gt-linear-conditional}.

1: \section{Measurement}
2: \label{sec:least-safe-measurement}
3: 
4: Every statement this paper has proved about the least safe modulus concerns the
5: divisors of $G(N)$: $\ell(N) > B$ exactly when every $m \le B$ divides $G(N)$, that is,
6: when $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) \mid G(N)$ (\cref{thm:least-safe-lcm-before,thm:least-safe-lt-of-icc-dvd}),
7: and $\ell(N)$ is the least non-divisor of $G(N)$ (\cref{def:least-safe-modulus}).
8: What is left is what that divisor set looks like in the numbers we can reach.
9: This section reports three measurements of it, each with the script and the range
10: that produced it: the size of
11: $\ell$ against the scale of the bounds of this paper; the levels $L \mid G(N)$
12: among the three-factor Carmichael numbers up to $10^{23}$, with the first member
13: of each level; and the size of the multiplier $K$ of
14: \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} at the conductors where it has been
15: computed. These are measurements, not theorems, and no theorem depends on them.
16: 
17: An odd composite's invariant divides $N(N-1)$, and on a Carmichael number
18: \cref{thm:least-safe-lcm-before} reads
19: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell(N)-1) \mid G(N)$. The cube inequality
20: $G(N)^3 \le N$, which holds on the squarefree numbers with at least three prime
21: factors and so on every Carmichael number, gives the other side
22: (\cite{Companion-residue-gcd}):
23: \[
24:   \ln\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell(N)-1) \;\le\; \ln G(N) \;\le\;
25:   \frac{1}{3}\ln N .
26: \]
27: That chain is the scale in which this section measures, and it is not derived
28: here. For squarefree $N$ with at least three prime factors the source states the
29: sharper form $\ell(N) \le \lfloor \log_2 N / 3 \rfloor + \log_2(\log_2 N + 2) + 3$
30: (\cite{Companion-residue-gcd},
31: \lean{NumeratorCalculus.leastSafeModulus\_le\_third\_log2}).
32: 
33: What a record measures is the level of the least common multiple, not the value
34: of $\ell$: an occurrence at $\ell$ means
35: $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\ell-1) \mid G(N)$, so a first occurrence at a level
36: $L$ belongs in the ratio $\ln N / \ln L$, which is the ratio reported. Over
37: every Carmichael number below $10^{18}$ --- Pinch's tables \cite{Pinch2006} ---
38: the first $N$ with $L \mid G(N)$ has
39: \[
40:   \frac{\ln N}{\ln L} \;=\;
41:   12.69,\ 14.62,\ 10.63,\ 8.96,\ 9.80,\ 10.17,\ 9.54,\ 10.00,\ 8.55
42: \]
43: at $L = 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, 60, 120$, while $L = 840$ is not attained below
44: $10^{18}$ (\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak results/\allowbreak
45: least\_safe\_constant.txt}). As a fraction of $\ln N$, the records of $\ell$
46: itself give $\ell/\ln N = 0.316, 0.341, 0.249, 0.198, 0.171$ at
47: $\ell = 2, 3, 4, 5, 7$, and normalised by $\ln N/\ln\ln N$ instead they read $0.58$ to $0.74$
48: (\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak results/\allowbreak
49: pinch\_least\_safe\_records.txt}); the census adds $0.151$ at $\ell = 8$. The
50: value $1/6 \approx 0.167$ lies inside that band, and the heuristic
51: $\ell \approx \ln N/6$ is a fit to those six records. In the other convention the
52: proved statement is the chain above, where the same data read $8.55$ to $14.62$;
53: the two stand in separate sentences, each with its own file. The records of
54: \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder} lie at the top of the band --- $\ln N/\ln L$ from
55: $12.6$ to $14.5$ over all seven rows --- on members that, at $\ell = 7$ and $8$, are not minimal for their level.
56: 
57: The census counts three-factor Carmichael numbers \cite{AGP1994} --- the $N = pqr$
58: with $p - 1$, $q - 1$ and $r - 1$ each dividing $N - 1$, which is Korselt's
59: criterion --- and asks which $L$ divide their $G$. The members are few,
60: which is why the census can be complete in its
61: range: \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak carmichael3\_G/\allowbreak carm3g.c}
62: reaches $10^{23}$ and finds $790885$ of them ($224763$ below $10^{21}$), \texttt{analyse.py} reduces each number to $G$ and to its level, and
63: \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak results/\allowbreak
64: carmichael3\_G\_1e23\_analysis.txt} tabulates the result. The $G$ column is read
65: on the CRT idempotents of $N$: at a Carmichael number every attainable residue is
66: an idempotent and every idempotent is attained, so $G(N)$ is the gcd of $e - 1$
67: over the nonzero idempotents $e$, which is the reading of
68: \cref{thm:least-safe-numerators}, and \texttt{verify\_records.py} rechecks the
69: record line independently of the C program: the three factors are prime, each
70: $p - 1$ divides $N - 1$, and the gcd of $e - 1$ over the nonzero idempotents is
71: $420$.
72: 
73: The level $420 \mid G$ --- that is $\ell = 8$, the second value of
74: \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder} --- has its first three-factor member at
75: \[
76:   N = 7757611 \cdot 20686961 \cdot 618022931 = 99181182856782173431201
77:   \;\approx\; 9.9 \cdot 10^{22},
78: \]
79: with $\ln N = 52.95$ and $\ln N/\ln 420 = 8.77$. The number is not a
80: member of the consecutive family at the conductor $m = 839$: the three quotients
81: $(p-1)/839$, $(q-1)/840$ and $(r-1)/841$ are not integers, while a member at $m = 839$ has $p - 1 = 839K$, $q - 1 = 840K$ and $r - 1 = 841K$. So the census finds a recorded level at a number the construction did not build. The
82: minimality claim travels with the value: it is the first three-factor member of
83: its level, and numbers with four or more factors are enumerated only to
84: $10^{18}$.
85: 
86: The third measurement is the size of the multiplier that
87: \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} asks for. At a conductor $m$ the
88: three divisibilities of that hypothesis put $K$ in one class modulo
89: $m(m+1)(m+2)$ --- the three moduli are pairwise coprime because $m$ is odd --- so
90: \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak least\_K\_lcm\_moduli.py} walks that class and
91: reports the least $K$ for which $mK+1$, $(m+1)K+1$ and $(m+2)K+1$ are prime,
92: with the exponent $\log K / \log\bigl(m(m+1)(m+2)\bigr)$ that the size bound
93: compares with $C$. At $m = 2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B) - 1$ that exponent
94: lies between $1.040$ and $1.809$ for every prime-power level $B \le 47$, so in
95: the form of \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size}, the hypothesis holds with $C = 2$ at every
96: level measured (\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak results/\allowbreak
97: least\_K\_lcm\_moduli.txt}). Its primalities are decided by Miller--Rabin on the
98: first thirteen prime bases, which is a proof of primality below
99: $3.3 \cdot 10^{24}$ \cite{SorensonWebster2017} and only a strong probable-prime
100: test above it, that is, at every level from $B = 13$ on; Rabin's error bound does
101: not apply there, since the bases are fixed rather than random.
102: 
103: The measurements settle nothing about the primality of the three values $mK+1$,
104: $(m+1)K+1$ and $(m+2)K+1$ that
105: \cref{def:least-safe-dickson-consec-lcm-size} needs. The measurements above are printed with the file that produced them, except the
106: ratios $\ln N/\ln L$ of the records, which are computed from \cref{tab:least-safe-ladder}.

15: \section{Checking the certificates}
16: \label{app:least-safe-certificates}
17: 
18: The nine factors of the last three records of
19: \cref{sec:least-safe-certificates} have between $26$ and $37$ digits, beyond
20: trial division, and each is certified prime by a finite certificate that the
21: kernel checks. The check is Pratt's criterion \cite{Pratt1975}, read through
22: Lucas's converse of Fermat's little theorem (\lean{lucas\_primality}), and it
23: follows the precedent of formally checked Pocklington certificates
24: \cite{TheryPrimality2022}. Nothing here is new mathematics: it is the arithmetic
25: that turns primality into a verdict the kernel can compute.
26: 
27: A certificate is either the one that names $2$, or a triple of a number $p$, a
28: base $a$ and a list of certificate-and-exponent pairs. The checker reads six
29: clauses of it: $p$ lies below a fixed fuel bound, every listed certificate is
30: accepted, every listed exponent is positive, the listed prime powers multiply to
31: $p - 1$, the computed residue of $a^{p-1}$ is $1$, and the computed residue of
32: $a^{(p-1)/q}$ differs from $1$ at every listed $q$.
33: 
34: Both residues are computed by binary exponentiation: the base is squared modulo
35: the modulus and the exponent halved at each step (\lean{binModPowFuel}), so that
36: halving a positive exponent halves its bit length (\lean{bitLength},
37: \lean{bitLength\_div2}), while an exponent below $2^k$ has bit length at most
38: $k$ (\lean{bitLength\_le\_of\_lt\_two\_pow}). With the fuel set to the bit length
39: of the exponent the procedure returns $a^n \bmod m$ for every $n$ and every
40: nonzero $m$ (\lean{binModPow}, \lean{binModPow\_eq}); in the residue ring modulo
41: a prime $p$ the same identity reads that the computed value is the class of
42: $a^n$ (\lean{binModPowFuel\_coe}), so that it is $1$ exactly when
43: $a^n \equiv 1 \pmod p$ (\lean{binModPowFuel\_eq\_one\_iff}). That identity is
44: the first of the appendix's two statements.
45: 
46: \begin{lemma}\lv\lean{binModPowFuel\_eq}
47: \label{lem:least-safe-binmodpow-fuel}
48: Let $a$, $n$ and $m \ne 0$ be natural numbers, and let the fuel $k$ be at least
49: the bit length of $n$. Then the exponentiation with fuel $k$, base $a$, exponent
50: $n$ and modulus $m$ returns $a^n \bmod m$.
51: \end{lemma}
52: \begin{proof}
53: Induct on the fuel. At exponent $0$, at any fuel, the procedure returns
54: $1 \bmod m$, which is $a^0 \bmod m$; and at fuel $0$ the exponent is $0$ in any
55: case, since the bit length of $0$ is $0$ and a positive number has positive bit
56: length. At fuel $k + 1$ with $n$ positive the procedure squares the base modulo
57: $m$ and recurses at the exponent $n/2$, whose bit length is half that of $n$
58: (\lean{bitLength\_div2}), so the remaining fuel still covers it and the induction
59: hypothesis applies to the recursive call, which returns
60: $\bigl((a \cdot a) \bmod m\bigr)^{n/2} \bmod m$. If $n$ is even this is
61: $(a^2)^{n/2} = a^n$ modulo $m$; if $n$ is odd the procedure multiplies by $a$,
62: reduced modulo $m$, and $2 \cdot (n/2) + 1 = n$ gives $a^n$ modulo $m$ again.
63: \end{proof}
64: 
65: Every clause of the check is one of these: the fuel guard (\lean{lucasFuel}); the
66: recursion through the list, each listed certificate accepted in its turn
67: (\lean{prattOKList}, \lean{prattOKList\_ok}); the positivity of the listed
68: exponents (\lean{expsPos}, \lean{expsPos\_pos}); the product of the listed prime
69: powers, nonzero as a product of prime powers and divisible by each listed value
70: (\lean{factorProd}, \lean{factorProd\_ne\_zero}, \lean{mem\_dvd\_factorProd});
71: and the two families of computed residues (\lean{prattNeList\_ne}). A prime
72: divisor of that product is one of the listed values
73: (\lean{factorProd\_prime\_dvd}), so each listed factor is prime by induction on
74: the list (\lean{prattOKList\_prime}), and the number a certificate names is its
75: own (\lean{PrattCert.value}). That an accepted certificate names a prime is the
76: second statement, \lean{prattOK\_prime} \lv, also exported as
77: \lean{prime\_of\_prattOK} \lv; the checker itself is \lean{prattOK}.
78: 
79: \begin{theorem}\lv\lean{prattOK\_prime}
80: \label{thm:least-safe-pratt-sound}
81: A certificate that \lean{prattOK} accepts names a prime.
82: \end{theorem}
83: \begin{proof}
84: The certificate that names only $2$ names the prime $2$. Otherwise acceptance
85: unwinds into the six clauses above. Each listed factor is prime: an induction on
86: the list, a listed certificate being accepted as soon as the whole list is
87: (\lean{prattOKList\_prime}). The list cannot be empty, an empty product being $1$
88: while the product must be $p - 1$; so it holds a factor $q$ with a positive
89: exponent $e$, and $2 \le q^e \le p - 1 < p$, whence $p > 1$. Since $p < 2^{128}$, the exponents
90: $p - 1$ and $(p-1)/q$ have bit length at most $128$
91: (\lean{bitLength\_le\_of\_lt\_two\_pow}), and with that much fuel the computed
92: residues are the classes of $a^{p-1}$ and of $a^{(p-1)/q}$ modulo $p$
93: (\lean{binModPowFuel\_eq\_one\_iff}); the two clauses therefore read
94: $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$ and $a^{(p-1)/q} \not\equiv 1 \pmod p$ at every
95: listed $q$. Lucas's converse (\lean{lucas\_primality}) asks this at every prime
96: $q$ dividing $p - 1$: such a $q$ divides the product of the listed prime powers,
97: hence equals one of the listed values, because those values are prime
98: (\lean{factorProd\_prime\_dvd}), and at that value the check has supplied the
99: non-congruence (\lean{prattNeList\_ne}). Hence $p$ is prime.
100: \end{proof}
